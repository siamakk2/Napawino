The Perfect 3-Day Napa Valley Itinerary

Napa Wino editors · Updated August 2026

Personalise this itinerary →

The single biggest itinerary mistake in Napa is zigzagging — the valley is one long corridor, and Highway 29 traffic punishes backtracking. This plan moves in one direction, south to north, with two to three tastings a day and lunch always placed between stops. Book every tasting ahead; most estates are appointment-only.

Day 1 — Napa & Carneros (the soft landing)

Start in the city of Napa, the valley's gateway and about 90 minutes from San Francisco. If you're up for it, board the Napa Valley Wine Train at the downtown depot — restored Pullman cars, lunch on white linen, vineyards sliding past. Otherwise go west into Carneros for morning bubbles and Pinot Noir on the terrace at Domaine Carneros, or hilltop views at Artesa. Lunch at Oxbow Public Market, then walk the Rail Arts District's murals. Late afternoon: ten minutes up the Silverado Trail to Darioush and its Persepolis-inspired columns. Evening: live music downtown at Napa Music Hall or the Uptown Theatre.

Day 2 — The Oakville–Rutherford icon corridor

This is the heart of Napa Cabernet. Morning tasting at Robert Mondavi in Oakville (the best history lesson in the valley) or, if you booked far ahead, Opus One next door. Lunch is the classic decision: burgers at Gott's Roadside in St. Helena if you're keeping it casual, or the hilltop terrace at Auberge du Soleil in Rutherford if you're celebrating. Afternoon: the ivy-covered château at Inglenook or the organic, unstuffy Frog's Leap. After three tastings, call a driver. Dinner in Yountville: Bistro Jeanty or Bouchon, or The French Laundry if you won that reservation months ago.

Day 3 — Calistoga: caves, castles, mud

Drive to the valley's northern end. Morning cave tour and sparkling flight at Schramsberg, where the tunnels were hand-dug in the 1870s — or cross the drawbridge at Castello di Amorosa if castles beat caves. Ride the aerial gondola at Sterling Vineyards for the valley's best panorama. Then do what Calistoga has been famous for since the 1860s: a volcanic mud bath and soak. If you'd rather move than soak, the Oat Hill Mine Trail climbs straight out of town.

Want to add a balloon?

Hot-air balloons launch at dawn near Yountville, so they only fit on a morning you've kept clear — slot it on day two, land by 9am, and pick the itinerary back up at Mondavi. Flights cancel for weather, so book early in your trip and leave a spare morning.

Three rules that save the trip

One: never more than three tastings a day — palate fatigue is real and so are DUI checkpoints; budget for a driver on day two. Two: book restaurants when you book flights; Yountville tables go fast. Three: check harvest timing — September and October (“the crush”) are magical but busy, so reserve everything earlier.
