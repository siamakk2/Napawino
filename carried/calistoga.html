Calistoga: Mud, Minerals & Castles

Hot springs since the 1860s · the valley's most theatrical wineries

Plan my Calistoga day →

The valley ends in steam. Calistoga sits on geothermal springs at Napa's volcanic northern tip — the original resort town, where the mud baths predate the wine fame by a century and the wineries come with moats, gondolas, and caves.

The vibe

Old-West main street meets spa town. Lincoln Avenue is walkable, unpretentious, and lined with the hot-springs resorts that made Calistoga famous in the 1860s. It's the quiet end of the valley — smaller crowds, cooler mornings, and the most distinctive day of any Napa trip: taste in a castle, soak in volcanic mud, sleep above a mineral pool.

The mud bath ritual

The classic sequence: warm volcanic-ash mud, a mineral hot-spring soak, then a blanket wrap while your bones turn to jelly. A dozen spas along and off Lincoln Avenue run the tradition, from vintage motor-court charm to full resort treatment at Solage. Book the spa for the afternoon after your tastings — it's the best recovery hour in wine country.

The theatrical wineries

No stretch of the valley shows off like this one. Château Montelena — the 1976 Judgment of Paris Chardonnay winner — pours above Jade Lake. Castello di Amorosa is a genuine medieval-style castle: moat, drawbridge, frescoed great hall. Schramsberg's candlelit caves were dug by hand in the 1870s (reservation essential; our current winery of the month). Sterling floats you up by aerial gondola, Frank Family keeps it warm and easy in the 1884 Larkmead stone winery, and Tamber Bey's horse ranch is the valley's most famously dog-friendly tasting. Pick three in the directory — or ask the sommelier to build the castle-cave-soak day.

Beyond the glass

The Oat Hill Mine Trail climbs straight out of town through volcanic rock — hike the lower stretch for the views. Old Faithful Geyser of California erupts on schedule just north, the Petrified Forest is a short drive west, and Robert Louis Stevenson State Park's Mount St. Helena summit trail crowns the valley. In winter, the Winter in the Wineries Passport (December–February) makes Calistoga the valley's best-value season, and November's lighted tractor parade is the local tradition worth planning around.

Eat & drink

Solbar at Solage holds Michelin recognition and a dog-friendly patio; Buster's Southern BBQ runs its oak-fired grill in the parking lot at the other end of the spectrum. Both are exactly right.

With your dog

Tamber Bey's Garden Bar, Solbar's patio, Buster's picnic tables, and voice-control hiking on the Oat Hill Mine Trail — Calistoga treats dogs well. The full map is in the dog-friendly guide.

Staying here

For spa-led trips, quiet, and the north-valley wineries at your doorstep. It's the longest drive from San Francisco and the best reward for making it. Compare all four towns in Where to Stay.
