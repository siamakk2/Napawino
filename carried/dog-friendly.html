Dog-Friendly Napa Valley: Wineries, Patios & Trails

Verified venue by venue · Official links included · Updated August 2026

Ask the sommelier →

Napa has quietly become one of California's most dog-friendly destinations — but "dogs welcome" means very different things at different gates, and two of the valley's most famous hiking parks don't allow dogs at all. Here's the honest, verified map.

The ground rules, first

Nearly everywhere in wine country, the deal is the same: dogs stay leashed and outdoors — patios, lawns, terraces, and gardens, not tasting rooms or cellars. California law allows leashed dogs in outdoor restaurant dining areas, which is why Napa's patio culture works so well for dog owners. Water bowls are common; treats are increasingly standard. Policies shift with heat, harvest, and events, so a 30-second call ahead is the difference between a great day and a U-turn.

Wineries where dogs are genuinely welcome

Up-valley & Calistoga. Tamber Bey at Sundance Ranch is the valley's most famously dog-friendly stop — a working horse ranch where leashed dogs join you at the Garden Bar. Clif Family in St. Helena has made pet hospitality core to the visit, and Raymond Vineyards is legendary among dog people (ask about the Frenchie connection). HALL St. Helena pairs contemporary art with dog-friendly grounds, and Whitehall Lane is a long-time favorite.

Rutherford & Oakville. St. Supéry is welcoming across the whole estate. Alpha Omega allows leashed dogs on its spacious terraces. Honig — solar-powered, family-run, and home to winery dogs of its own — and Frog's Leap's red barn and organic gardens round out the friendliest cluster in the valley's heart. Mumm Napa's sparkling terrace and Napa Cellars' picnic grounds both welcome leashed dogs.

Napa, Stags Leap & Carneros. Clos Du Val and Trefethen are reliably dog-friendly classics. Etude in Carneros has a huge lawn made for a sprawling afternoon — and it's the closest dog-friendly stop to San Francisco. Downtown, WALT's tasting room across from Oxbow keeps things walkable.

The picnic king. V. Sattui in St. Helena — walk-ins, a full deli, and two acres of picnic grounds — is the single easiest dog day in Napa Valley. Leash on, sandwich in hand, done.

Restaurant patios, from Michelin to milkshakes

At the top: Bouchon in Yountville and Solbar at Solage in Calistoga both hold Michelin recognition and both seat leashed dogs on their patios — yes, you can do a starred lunch with your dog. Farmstead at Long Meadow Ranch (St. Helena) is the farm-to-table move; Angèle on the Napa riverfront and Bistro Jeanty in Yountville handle the French end; Morimoto's river-walk patio covers the splurgy sushi night.

Casual and downtown: Gott's Roadside (the St. Helena lawn is all outdoor seating; the Oxbow location has a patio), Napa General Store at the Napa Mill, Bounty Hunter for BBQ and 40 wines by the glass, Grace's Table, Compline for the wine-nerd dinner, Boon Fly Café in Carneros for the doughnut breakfast, Ca' Momi Osteria for Italian, Sky & Vine rooftop for sunset, and Buster's Southern BBQ in Calistoga, where the oak-fired grill runs in the parking lot and picnic tables await. At Oxbow Public Market, dog seating is the patio on the parking-lot side, with an outdoor order window.

Trails & parks — where dogs actually can go

This is where guidebooks get it wrong most often. The real list:

Yes, dogs allowed: Alston Park in Napa — 157 acres of rolling grassland ringed by vineyard, with a designated off-leash area and the Canine Commons dog park; hot-air balloons literally land here some mornings. The Oat Hill Mine Trail from Calistoga — a historic wagon road climbing through volcanic rock with big valley views; dogs under voice control or leash. Moore Creek Park (Lake Hennessey unit) — leashed, and strictly no contact with the lake (it's Napa's drinking water). And the Napa Valley Vine Trail — the paved valley-long path, perfect for leashed walks between towns, including the stretch through Bothe.

No dogs on trails — despite what you may have read: Skyline Wilderness Park, Robert Louis Stevenson State Park (the Mount St. Helena climb), Bale Grist Mill, and the dirt trails of Bothe-Napa Valley State Park (dogs are fine in Bothe's campground, day-use, and paved areas — they even have dog-friendly yurts).

A perfect Napa dog day

Morning off-leash hour at Alston Park → picnic and a bottle at V. Sattui → afternoon garden tasting at Tamber Bey or Frog's Leap → early patio dinner at Farmstead or Angèle.
