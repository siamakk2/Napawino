St. Helena: The Historic Heart

Stone wineries, Main Street & the valley's best picnic

Plan my St. Helena day →

If Napa Valley has a hometown, it's this one: stone wineries from the 1800s bookend a Main Street that still feels like a main street. St. Helena is where the valley's history lives — and where its best picnic happens.

The vibe

Elegant but unhurried. Highway 29 becomes Main Street for a few tree-lined blocks of boutiques, tasting rooms, and restaurants, then returns to vines. The 19th-century stone wineries — some of the oldest in California — sit minutes from town in every direction, and the hills close in enough that the valley feels intimate here in a way the wide southern end doesn't.

Winery row

History first: Beringer (1876, tour the Rhine House), Charles Krug (the valley's first, 1861), and the 1884 stone cellar at Frank Family just north. Icons next: Duckhorn's veranda, Joseph Phelps' terrace above the Insignia vineyard, Heitz, Louis M. Martini, and small family benchmarks Spottswoode and Corison. Contemporary counterweight: HALL's glass winery and its giant chrome rabbit. And the crowd-pleaser: V. Sattui — walk-ins daily, full deli, two acres of picnic lawn. Assemble your three in the directory or hand it to the sommelier.

Eat & drink

Gott's Roadside's original lawn does the burger-and-shake lunch; Farmstead at Long Meadow Ranch does farm-to-table in a former nursery barn (great dog patio); The Charter Oak cooks over live fire; and Michelin-starred PRESS holds a 3,000-bottle Napa cellar where the cult wines you can't visit occasionally surface by the glass.

Beyond the glass

Five minutes north, Bale Grist Mill's 36-foot waterwheel still turns, and Bothe-Napa Valley State Park hides actual redwoods along Ritchey Canyon — the valley's most surprising hike. The Vine Trail stretch here rolls flat through the vines toward Calistoga, and harvest season smells better in St. Helena than anywhere else.

With your dog

V. Sattui's picnic grounds, Gott's lawn, Farmstead's patio, and Clif Family's pet-first tasting room make St. Helena arguably the best dog town in the valley. Note: Bothe's dirt trails are no-dog (paved areas and campground are fine). Full rules in the dog-friendly guide.

Staying here

Closest base to the most historic wineries, with a real town attached. Quieter than Napa, less scene-y than Yountville, and the shortest drives to the valley's north half. Compare in Where to Stay.

Base in St. Helena and tell our sommelier what you love — it routes the historic row so lunch lands right on Main Street.
