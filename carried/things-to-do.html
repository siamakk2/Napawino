Things to Do in Napa Beyond Wine

Balloons, trails, music & the deals locals use · Updated August 2026

Ask the sommelier →

A Napa trip built only on tastings burns out by day two. The valley's best days mix a dawn balloon, a real hike, a long lunch, one great tasting, and music after dark. Here's the full menu — with the passes that quietly cut your costs.

Up at dawn: the balloon ride

Flights launch around sunrise — calm air, soft light, the fog pulling off the vines — and last about an hour aloft, usually from the Yountville area. The established operators are Napa Valley Balloons, Napa Valley Aloft, and Balloons Above the Valley; most include a post-flight sparkling toast or breakfast. Book ahead in summer and during mustard season, dress in layers, and know that pilots scrub flights for wind — build in a backup morning if the balloon is your trip's centerpiece. Budget roughly $250–$400 per person. (Fun fact: they sometimes land at Alston Park, where balloon landings are officially sanctioned.)

Real hiking, honestly rated

Skyline Wilderness Park (Napa) — the locals' park: 3-to-8-mile loops through oak groves and wildflower grasslands (note: no dogs on trails). Robert Louis Stevenson State Park — the big one: the Mount St. Helena climb, about 10 miles round-trip to a summit that sees the whole North Bay (also no dogs). Oat Hill Mine Trail from Calistoga — a 19th-century wagon road cut through volcanic rock; hike the lower stretch for views without the full commitment (dogs OK). Alston Park — easy 2.7-mile grassland loop, spring wildflowers, off-leash dog area. Moore Creek Park at Lake Hennessey — quiet oak-woodland miles east of St. Helena. And the Napa Valley Vine Trail — the flat, paved, valley-long path that's the single best way to move between towns on two wheels; rent bikes in Napa or Yountville and ride to Yountville for lunch. Full trail details in our dog-friendly guide if you're bringing a pup.

Music & nights out

Downtown Napa carries the valley's nightlife. The Uptown Theatre — a restored 1937 Art Deco house — books national touring acts year-round; JaM Cellars Ballroom runs the club-scale shows; and the open-air Oxbow RiverStage brings big summer concerts to the riverbank. The giant is BottleRock: three days every Memorial Day weekend at the Napa Valley Expo — 75+ artists on five stages, plus the culinary stage where chefs and rock stars share knives, wrapped in the best festival food and wine in America (~120,000 attend; lodging sells out months out). In July, Festival Napa Valley fills wineries and lawns with classical, opera, and dance. See what's booked this week on our live music & dinners page.

The rest of the greatest hits

Calistoga mud baths & hot springs — the valley's original attraction since the 1860s; volcanic mud, then a mineral soak. Oxbow Public Market — Napa's food hall and the best cheap-eats hour in the valley. The Napa Valley Wine Train — see our honest review of who it's actually for. Di Rosa Center for Contemporary Art in Carneros and the art collections at HALL and The Hess Persson estates cover the culture stop. And in late winter, the mustard bloom turns every vineyard row neon yellow — the valley's most photogenic free attraction.

The events calendar, by season

Winter (Dec–Feb): Calistoga's Winter in the Wineries passport season, the Napa Truffle Festival (January), the Napa Lighted Art Festival downtown, and restaurant-week deals — the valley at its quietest and cheapest. Spring (Mar–Apr): mustard season fades into bud break; tasting rooms are relaxed before the rush. Late May: BottleRock takes over. Summer (Jun–Aug): outdoor concert season at RiverStage, Festival Napa Valley in July, long warm evenings in Yountville. Fall (Aug–Oct): harvest — crush pads running, grape smell in the air, the valley at its most alive (and most booked). November: the quirkiest local tradition, Calistoga's lighted tractor parade, closes the year. Full current dates on our events page.

Deals & passes that actually save money

Tasting fees run $40–$100+ per person, so the pass math matters. The Napa Vine Pass (~$60, valid all of 2026) unlocks discounted tastings and bottle deals at 23 sommelier-picked boutique wineries — the best fit for travelers who want small, personal rooms. The Priority Wine Pass (~$69) is the volume play: 400+ wineries across Napa and Sonoma with frequent 2-for-1 tastings. UnCorked St. Helena bundles 10 wineries in a seasonal passport, and Calistoga's Winter in the Wineries Passport (December–February) remains the classic off-season bargain. Beyond passes: many wineries waive fees with bottle purchases, sharing one flight between two people is normal and unashamed, and Michelin dining is dramatically cheaper at lunch. Visit midweek or in winter and everything above gets easier to book and gentler on the card.
