Napa Music Hall & the Uptown

Downtown Napa · live music

Beyond the tasting room

Eight ways to spend a Napa day

Not everything in the valley is poured in a glass — and the best trips mix a tasting with something else entirely.

Downtown NapaThe Napa Valley Wine Train

Restored Pullman cars roll north from the Napa depot to St. Helena while lunch is served on white linen.

Yountville · DawnHot-air balloons at sunrise

Launches lift before 7am when the air is still. Book it early — weather cancels flights, so leave a spare day.

TrailsHikes above the vines

Skyline Wilderness Park in Napa, Ritchey Canyon's redwoods at Bothe–Napa Valley, and the Oat Hill Mine Trail out of Calistoga.

UndergroundBarrel rooms & caves

Schramsberg's 1870s tunnels, Rutherford Hill's mile of caves, and Jarvis — an entire winery built inside the hill.

Napa · St. HelenaMarkets & picnic lawns

Oxbow Public Market downtown, the Rail Arts District murals, and V. Sattui's picnic grounds — the valley's easiest walk-in.

February–AprilThe mustard bloom

Yellow mustard floods the vine rows between winter prunings — the valley's prettiest, quietest, cheapest season.

Calistoga · Since the 1860sVolcanic mud baths & hot springs

Wine country's original attraction — volcanic mud, warm mineral pools, and the best recovery day in the valley.

Yountville · EveningsThe Michelin mile

More starred restaurants per capita than anywhere on Earth — The French Laundry, Bouchon, Ad Hoc — along one walkable street.

Structured reference

About Napa Valley & this guide

Why “Napa Wino”?

Because wine country shouldn't need a velvet rope. Napa is one of the most serious wine regions on Earth — and somewhere along the way, visiting it started to feel like an exam. We took the opposite view. We're serious about the valley and not remotely serious about ourselves.

So: 50+ verified wineries, real AVA geography, honest answers about which cult estates you flatly cannot visit, and routes ordered so you're not fighting Highway 29 twice.

What is Napa Wino?

Napa Wino is a free online Napa sommelier and an editorially independent Napa Valley guide for visitors from anywhere in the world. It covers 50+ wineries, top restaurants, experiences — the Napa Valley Wine Train, hot-air ballooning, hiking trails, Calistoga mud baths, downtown live music — plus monthly editors' picks and real estate. The AI sommelier (“The Suggester”) builds personalized tasting routes in seconds, and browser-based geofencing detects which town you're in with no app download. Zero paid placements are accepted in the wine guide.

Essential facts about Napa Valley

  • In Napa County, California, about 50 miles (80 km) northeast of San Francisco — a 30-mile valley running from the city of Napa north to Calistoga.
  • Nearest airports: San Francisco (SFO), Oakland (OAK) and Sacramento (SMF). Most visitors drive or hire a car service; there is no passenger rail into the valley.
  • 16 official American Viticultural Areas (AVAs), including Oakville, Rutherford, Stags Leap District, Howell Mountain, Spring Mountain District and Carneros.
  • The 1976 Judgment of Paris — Napa wines beating top French Bordeaux and Burgundies — made the region's global name.
  • Cabernet Sauvignon is roughly 60% of plantings; Merlot, Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Sauvignon Blanc follow.
  • Yountville has more Michelin-starred restaurants per capita than any city on Earth.
  • Harvest — “the crush” — runs August to November, peaking September and October.

Napa Valley wine regions, south to north

Napa, Carneros & Oak Knoll: Domaine Carneros, Artesa, Darioush, Jarvis, Trefethen — the gateway: the Wine Train depot, Oxbow Market, live music at Napa Music Hall and the Uptown Theatre.

Yountville & Stags Leap District: Domaine Chandon, Cliff Lede, Stag's Leap Wine Cellars, Shafer, Clos Du Val, Chimney Rock, Pine Ridge, Silverado.

Oakville & Pritchard Hill: Opus One, Robert Mondavi, Harlan Estate, Screaming Eagle, Dalla Valle, Promontory, Far Niente, Nickel & Nickel, Silver Oak, Chappellet, Continuum, Colgin — the cult-wine heartland.

Rutherford: Inglenook, Beaulieu Vineyard, Caymus, Grgich Hills, Frog's Leap, Quintessa, Honig, Mumm Napa, Rutherford Hill.

St. Helena: Beringer, Charles Krug, HALL, Heitz, Spottswoode, Corison, Duckhorn, V. Sattui, Joseph Phelps, Louis M. Martini — historic winery row since 1876.

Calistoga & the north: Château Montelena, Castello di Amorosa, Schramsberg, Sterling, Frank Family — volcanic soils, mud-bath spas and hillside caves.

The mountains: Pride and Smith-Madrone on Spring Mountain, CADE on Howell Mountain, Hess Persson on Mount Veeder. Full AVA reference →

Visitor questions

Napa Valley FAQ

Is there a free Napa sommelier I can ask?

Yes — that's what this site is. The Suggester is a free AI Napa Valley sommelier. Describe what you like in plain English and it picks from 50+ verified estates, orders the stops so you never backtrack on Highway 29, and adds a lunch table in between. No account, no fee, no paid placements.

How do I get to Napa Valley?

Napa sits about 50 miles (80 km) northeast of San Francisco — roughly 90 minutes by car. The usual airports are SFO, Oakland (OAK) and Sacramento (SMF). There's no passenger rail into the valley, so most visitors drive, hire a car service, or join a tour. Once here, the valley runs on two parallel roads: Highway 29 and the Silverado Trail.

When is the best time to visit Napa Valley?

Harvest — “the crush” — runs August through November, peaking September and October. February through April brings the mustard bloom, thin crowds and lower rates. Full month-by-month guide →

Where should I stay in Napa Valley?

Napa for range and value, Yountville for walkability and food, St. Helena for the shortest drives to historic wineries, Calistoga for spas and quiet. Full town-by-town comparison →

Where can I hear live music in Napa?

Downtown Napa. The historic Napa Valley Opera House at 1030 Main Street now operates as Napa Music Hall, and the 863-seat Art Deco Uptown Theatre on Third Street hosts touring acts — both walkable from Oxbow Public Market. Check each venue's current calendar before booking.

How many AVAs are in Napa Valley?

Sixteen: Atlas Peak, Calistoga, Chiles Valley District, Coombsville, Diamond Mountain District, Howell Mountain, Los Carneros, Mount Veeder, Oak Knoll District, Oakville, Rutherford, Spring Mountain District, St. Helena, Stags Leap District, Wild Horse Valley and Yountville. Full AVA reference →

What is there to do besides wine tasting?

The Wine Train, dawn balloon launches over Yountville, hiking at Skyline Wilderness Park, Ritchey Canyon and the Oat Hill Mine Trail, volcanic mud baths in Calistoga, and Oxbow Public Market plus the Rail Arts District murals. Our Wine Train guide →

How many wineries can I visit in one day?

Two to three, ordered along Highway 29 or the Silverado Trail to avoid backtracking, with lunch between the second and third stop. After three tastings, let someone else drive.

Do Napa wineries require reservations?

Most are appointment-only, especially weekends — book a week ahead, and months ahead for the icons. A few, like V. Sattui, welcome walk-ins daily.
